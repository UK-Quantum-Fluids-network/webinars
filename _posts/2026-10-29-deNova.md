The field of analogue gravity simulates otherwise inaccessible gravitational phenomena in laboratory setups [1]. This has eventually led to the first observation 
of the Hawking effect [2], using atomic condensates, where one can also study the analogue of the Andreev reflection [3]. One of the biggest remaining challenges is 
the observation of the black-hole laser (BHL) effect, in which Hawking radiation is exponentially self-amplified due to successive internal reflections between two 
horizons [4]. Of particular interest is the dynamical pase diagram of a BHL, after the initial instability has saturated, where it may exhibit an intriguing periodic state of 
continuous emission of solitons (CES), representing the bona fide BHL. In fact, the CES state is a universal feature of flowing condensates, which has inspired the more 
general concept of spontaneous Floquet state (SFS): a state of a time-independent Hamiltonian which oscillates like a Floquet state due to interactions [5].

We discuss how SFS provide a new paradigm of quantum matter [6]. For instance, due to energy conservation, they present a novel conserved charge, the 
Floquet charge, whose thermodynamic conjugate is the oscillation frequency. This allows to characterize spontaneous and driven Floquet states as "isofloquetic" or 
"isoperiodic", in analogy to standard isochoric and isobaric thermodynamic systems. Another hallmark of a SFS is the presence of a temporal Floquet-NambuGoldstone (FNG) mode with zero quasifrequency, whose quantum amplitude 
represents a rare tangible realization of time operator in Quantum Mechanics. The CES state is then a time supersolid, breaking U(1) and time-translation symmetry. 

Finally, we demonstrate how a bona fide time crystal, exhibiting a genuinely spontaneous symmetry breaking of time-translation symmetry, can be achieved in 
the CES state emerging from a quantum BHL [7]. Such a Hawking time crystal provides a nonlinear periodic analogue of the Andreev-Hawking effect. Remarkably, 
we prove that a time operator can be defined in any parametric amplifier, which enables a unique understanding of a time-crystallization process in terms of two 
time operators: one for the initial BHL and another for the final CES state.
